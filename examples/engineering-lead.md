## PROJECT HIGHLIGHTS

### Splitting the Monolith — Order and Fulfilment

Deploys had become a queue: eleven teams shared one release train, and a bad change from any of them blocked everyone. Led an **18-month** split of the order and fulfilment domains into **22 services** across **9 bounded contexts**, peeling out one context at a time and dual-writing during each cut-over so nothing went dark. Release cadence went from **fortnightly to on-demand**, and teams stopped coordinating deploys with each other.

### Rebuilding the Interview Loop

Offer-accept rate was **48%** and two hires in a row left within a year. Rewrote the loop around a work-sample exercise reviewed blind, replaced the whiteboard round, and wrote a scoring rubric interviewers actually filled in. Time-to-offer dropped from **five weeks to twelve days**, and offer-accept reached **81%** over the following 18 months.

### Cutting the On-Call Burden

The team was paged **60-70 times a month** and two engineers were close to leaving over it. Spent a quarter treating alert noise as a project rather than a chore: deleted alerts nobody acted on, replaced threshold alerts on symptoms with alerts on customer impact, and required every remaining alert to name a runbook. Pages fell to **under 15 a month** with no increase in missed incidents, measured against customer-reported outages.
